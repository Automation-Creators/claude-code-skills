# Revenue Content Strategy

## The claim (Hormozi's, from his own channel; tell the user it's unverified for them)

- His six most-viewed videos in a quarter (350K–1.2M views, beginner content) made **zero sales**.
- His six top-revenue videos that quarter had ~127K–438K views each, all aimed at people who already
  run businesses: a pricing/"where the money is" whiteboard essay, two live teardowns of real
  multi-million-dollar businesses, a money-mindset essay with value for beginners and experts alike,
  a payment-structure framework, and a straight Q&A for service-business owners.
- A quarter of deliberately broader content broke his view records while book sales, leads, and
  applications all fell.
- A registered dietitian with <6K followers and ~20 likes per post did >$1M/yr posting only about
  billing insurance as a dietitian.

> "The algorithm will tell you what the most people like, not the most valuable people like."

## Step 1 — Decide what the content is for

Ask: does the user sell ads/sponsorships (media business → views are the product), or use content to
get customers (→ buyers are the product)? Only the second case uses this skill.

## Step 2 — Define the buyer

- **Has customers:** take the top 20% by spend. List what they have in common, the words they used,
  and the problems they bought to solve. Topics come from those problems.
- **No customers yet:** use a proxy and say so: the target vertical's verbatim pains (reviews,
  forums, sales-call notes), plus who can afford the back-end offer. Replace the proxy once real
  buyers exist.

## Step 3 — Split the plan into two lanes

| Lane | Made for | Judged on | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| `revenue` | the buyer (already has the problem and the budget) | UTM clicks per 1K views, opt-ins, calls, $ | ≥1 in 3 long-form pieces to start |
| `reach` | a broad audience | views, follows, watch time | the rest |

Tag every piece with its lane before it's made. Low views on a revenue piece are expected and are not,
on their own, a reason to kill the format.

## Step 4 — The vertical-value test (every reach piece)

"Would a beginner **and** an experienced, budget-holding buyer both get something from this?"
- Yes → keep. This is the goal ("qual" = quality + volume).
- Beginner-only → rewrite so the buyer gets value too, or keep it as reach-only and expect no sales.

## Step 5 — Pick a buyer format (structure only, never copy wording)

1. **Whiteboard-math essay:** counterintuitive thesis + proof in the first 30s → one visual model
   carried through → round-number math → a rule of thumb → a personal story → tactics.
2. **Live teardown of a real business:** cold-open problem → interview with real numbers →
   whiteboard triage (supply vs demand constraint, then the lever) → prioritized fixes → recap.
   Needs a real, consenting business.
3. **Q&A for the buyer vertical:** proof stack → 5–6 mini consults, each a diagnosis plus a rule.
4. **Framework essay with vertical value:** a ranked list or ladder that places both beginners and
   experts on it.

Titles name a buyer type or a money/stage level ("If you have a service business…", "the 1 to 10
million problem"), not a beginner how-to.

## Step 6 — Wire tracking before publishing

- One CTA per piece to a free diagnostic or lead magnet, dropped mid-video at a topic hinge (his
  pattern: ~15–35% in), with the paid next step (a call) behind the opt-in.
- UTM on every link: `utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=<organic channel>&utm_campaign=<piece id>&utm_content=<offer or magnet id>`.
- Log per piece: lane, views, link clicks, opt-ins, calls booked, $ closed. Use the project's results
  tracker if one exists.

## Step 7 — Verdict

After ~12 long-form pieces, compare lanes. The claim predicts reach wins on views and revenue wins
on clicks, opt-ins, calls and dollars. If the user's data disagrees, their data wins; say so and
rebalance.

## Output

A lane-tagged content plan (topic, lane, buyer problem it solves, format, vertical-value yes/no, CTA,
UTM) plus the tracking columns. For an audit of existing content: the same table filled from their
published pieces, with the revenue gaps called out.

## Guardrails

- Never state Hormozi's numbers as the user's expected results.
- Never invent buyer pains or proof. Pains come with a source; proof is real or omitted.
- Teardown content needs the featured business's consent.
